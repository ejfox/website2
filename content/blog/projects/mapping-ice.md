A dark, terminal-aesthetic map of publicly available ICE and immigration-enforcement facilities, where every source is graded with the NATO Admiralty code.

I didn't reach for the Admiralty code because this needed military-grade rigor. I reached for it because it's already how I think when I'm doing my own OSINT — reliability of the source on one axis, credibility of the information on the other — and it's a system built to carry exactly the ratings I care about. Borrowing it beats inventing a grading scheme, and it's dense: two characters tell you how much to trust a line.

It's also cosplay, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. I like how it looks. The dark terminal, the alphanumeric confidence codes — they make the thing feel like an intelligence product, which is partly borrowed seriousness and partly just an aesthetic I enjoy.

A bit of cosplay and a bit of utilitarianism, mixed together.
