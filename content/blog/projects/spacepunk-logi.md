Spacepunk started as a joke about enterprise software: a space-trucking game where the real enemy isn't pirates, it's the corporate nightmare of interstellar logistics. You run your hauls through deliberately terrible software — forms within forms, loading spinners that lie, error messages that blame you. The frustration is the mechanic.

But what I actually got pulled into is bigger than the joke. It's kinda more about using LLMs to generate an entire world of interactions — an economy, relationships, lifetimes, characters who go on existing whether you're watching or not. It's server-authoritative and persistent, with a gossip network that spreads rumors between NPCs across seven stations. The dream is a place that runs itself.

![Spacepunk NPC portrait](https://res.cloudinary.com/ejf/image/upload/projects/spacepunk/npc-6.png)

I'll be honest about where it actually is. Right now it's a lot of stats and not a lot of simulated life yet — you can watch the machinery run, the economy ticking, the relationships sitting there as numbers, but the thing I'm really chasing, the moment a world feels alive on its own, hasn't quite happened. That's the hard part, and it's the part I actually care about.
